Weight-Covariance Spectral Optimizer — Experiment Results

Comparison of our p×p weight-covariance spectral filter vs Adam on three MNIST variants. Best HP from sweep: rank=50, decay=0.9, lr=5e-4, update_every=50.

Summary: The optimizer successfully blocks random-label memorization (loss stays at ln(10) ≈ 2.303, exactly chance). However, it also hurts real-label learning — Adam outperforms by 2-5% across datasets. The spectral projection (rank 50 out of 235K params) is too aggressive, discarding useful gradient signal. The generalization gap (train−test) is much smaller for ours, but at the cost of underfitting.

Key Observations

Random labels — complete blocking: Our optimizer's loss stays at exactly 2.3025 (= ln(10), uniform over 10 classes) for all 20 epochs. The spectral filter correctly identifies that there's no consistent weight co-variation structure to exploit, and the gradients project to essentially zero. Adam slowly begins memorizing (14.5% train by epoch 20).
Underfitting on real data: On standard MNIST, our optimizer plateaus at ~94.3% test accuracy by epoch 1 and doesn't improve over 20 epochs. Adam reaches 97.1%. The rank-50 projection out of 235K parameters keeps only 0.02% of the gradient space. This is likely too aggressive — legitimate learning signals are being discarded.
Noisy features don't hurt Adam much: Adam's test accuracy drops only 0.2% (97.1% → 96.9%) when 784 noise features are added. The network learns to mostly ignore them. Our optimizer drops 1.9% (94.3% → 92.4%), possibly because the noise features add confusion to the covariance structure.
Near-zero generalization gap: Our optimizer maintains train ≈ test across all datasets. This is the regularization story working as intended — but the absolute performance cost is too high. The filter needs to be less aggressive: higher rank, softer projection (filter_strength < 1), or adaptive rank selection.
