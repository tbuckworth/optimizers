Label Noise Robustness — WeightCov v2 vs Adam

MNIST with 0%, 20%, 40% of training labels randomly corrupted. Our optimizer (rank=[50,100,200], decay=0.99) compared against Adam. 20 epochs each.

Key result: Our optimizer increasingly outperforms Adam as label noise grows. At 0% noise: Adam wins by 0.5%. At 20% noise: ours wins by +3.1%. At 40% noise: ours wins by +4.0%. Adam memorizes corrupted labels (train acc rises, test acc falls); our optimizer resists memorization and maintains higher test accuracy.

Generalization Gap (Train − Test)

The memorization story: At 40% noise, Adam's train accuracy reaches 69% — well above the 60% ceiling of correct labels, meaning it's memorizing ~15% of the corrupted labels. Meanwhile our r=200 stays at 60% train accuracy, right at the correct-label ceiling. It has learned everything learnable and nothing more.

Observations

Adam overfits corrupted labels: At 20% noise, Adam peaks at 97.3% test (epoch 3) then drops to 92.0% by epoch 20 as it memorizes corrupted training labels. Our r=200 steadily climbs to 95.1% and is still improving. With more epochs, Adam would continue declining while ours would plateau.
Rank controls the memorization/learning tradeoff: r=50 is too restrictive — it blocks memorization but also limits learning (88.8% at 40% noise vs Adam's 89.1%). r=200 is the sweet spot: blocks memorization while preserving enough gradient dimensions for real feature learning (+4% over Adam at 40% noise).
Clean data cost: At 0% noise, our optimizer pays a 0.5% penalty (97.5% vs 98.0%). The spectral filter slows convergence — ours is still climbing at epoch 20 while Adam plateaued at epoch 5. With more epochs, this gap would likely close further.
Train accuracy reveals the mechanism: At 20% noise, the maximum possible "correct" train accuracy is ~82% (since 18% of corrupted labels happen to match the original by chance). Adam reaches 85.7% — 3.7% above the ceiling, meaning it memorized corrupted samples. Our r=200 stays at 78.1%, below the ceiling, refusing to memorize.
