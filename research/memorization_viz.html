Memorization vs Generalization: Adam vs the Cov-Filter

Two-moons with 40% of training labels flipped. Same MLP, same data, two optimizers. Top row = training set (noisy labels — flipped points ringed); bottom row = clean test set. Scrub through training and watch Adam (left) carve islands around the flipped points — train accuracy climbs toward 1.0 while its test boundary degrades — while the Cov-Filter (right) keeps a smooth boundary, refuses to fit the noise (train plateaus at the clean ceiling ≈0.6), and holds test accuracy. The spectral filter's benefit is a high-dimensional effect; this 2D replay is the smallest setting where it shows.
