# Aggregate projection through J-Lens: bounded design assessment

**Recommendation:** test whether a many-example covariance filter improves an independently checked aggregate, then decode that actual aggregate. Do not substitute individual-PC descriptions, an equal-weight PC sum, or another word-list popularity test. This is source/metadata/design work only: no feature arrays, inference, decoder calls, tokenizer calls, readers or prior stages were executed. The research-workflow skill's construct-validity check motivates separating an operation smoke test from a useful empirical endpoint; root retains protocol and launch ownership.

## Exact operation and scope

For fit residual gradients g_i in R^1024, fit centered covariance and freeze its top k orthonormal columns U. For each independent query batch Q, retain its arithmetic mean ḡ_Q and coefficients α=Uᵀḡ_Q; form p_Q=Uα. Decode −p_Q versus −ḡ_Q through the same frozen J-Lens. Do not normalize individual gradients before covariance or averaging, equalize coefficients, subtract the fit mean from the query aggregate, or add that mean back. Those define different estimands. Store all norms, coefficients, raw/projected vectors and discarded components; sign selection is the fixed loss-descent convention, not a post-outcome choice.

Activation analogue: fit a separate covariance to fit h_i at the same token role, then compare h̄_Q with P_h h̄_Q, with positive display sign. Center only the covariance estimator; do not silently replace this by μ_h+P_h(h̄_Q−μ_h). A large persistent mean can be absent from a centered covariance subspace. That is a substantive possible failure, not a numerical defect to fix afterward.

**One preregistered secondary counterpart is worthwhile:** from the exact same unnormalized fit gradients, fit the uncentered second moment M=(1/N)Σg_i g_iᵀ=C+m_fit m_fitᵀ, where C uses the same population denominator N. Freeze its top **k=32** span V and compare V Vᵀḡ_Q using the identical query/reference batches, error controls, signs and decoder convention. Apply the same single counterpart to the secondary activation analogue. This costs no additional feature acquisition and directly tests a strong version of the intended shared-signal filter: centered covariance ignores a persistent mean, whereas M includes its energy. It is still a coefficient-preserving projection, not affine mean restoration. The centered-covariance projection remains the sole primary; a second-moment win cannot be relabelled its success. A difference between these two fixed operators isolates the consequence of including the empirical mean outer product in subspace fitting, but not a unique semantic mechanism or proof that the fitted mean is generalizable. M can also favor a generic/common offset or noise; independent references, zero/fit-mean controls and retained norms remain necessary. No extra ranks, post-outcome choice between operators, or further variants.

These are residual-coordinate gradients, identified with vectors under the Euclidean metric. Summing them represents a common additive residual perturbation across contexts at the chosen token role. They are not full parameter gradients. J maps residual directions forward; it is neither a parameter-gradient decoder nor a substitute for the actual backward gradient computation. Its 233 fitting prompts estimate averaged downstream Jacobians, not the new covariance or aggregate.

## Smallest useful many-example panel

Propose 1,792 natural-text observations: 1,024 covariance-fit, 256 query (16 fixed batches of 16), and 512 independent reference, one model and block 11 only. Use the same declared sampling distribution and loss/token-role rule in all roles. Freeze document-disjoint fit/query/reference membership and query batches before model outputs; report document dependence if several nonoverlapping spans come from one article. Do not count spans as independent articles. Split the reference into two fixed 256-row groups for a transparent sensitivity check, not outcome-dependent reference selection.

Assign roles by a frozen randomized rule over deduplicated article groups, not by making cached train/validation/test membership synonymous with experimental role. Capacity must establish enough distinct eligible article groups for the chosen split; if multiple spans per article are needed, predeclare their allocation/weighting and report article counts and dependence separately from the 1,792 observation count. The source audit may require revising sample counts before any acquisition is released; metadata paragraph-row totals alone are not admission evidence.

Concrete loss proposal: take a fixed 32-token context and the next eight observed tokens from each eligible natural span, no BOS/padding/truncation; mean teacher-forced NLL over those eight tokens. Differentiate with respect to the block-11 residual at context token 32, and save that activation too. This retains the successful completion-gradient capture contract but changes from tiny authored verb prefixes to a declared natural continuation population. Eligibility, document ownership, exact token IDs and source offsets must be frozen at a whole-panel preflight. No common artificial target and no label-based candidate selection.

## Endpoint that can fail meaningfully

Primary question: **does projection give a better estimate of the population's aggregate residual loss gradient at the same query budget?** Let r be the unfiltered reference mean. Compare the average over all 16 query batches of ‖p_Q−r‖² versus ‖ḡ_Q−r‖², without norm restoration. Require a positive raw-minus-projected improvement against each of the two pre-fixed reference groups for a bounded positive; retain the pooled-reference effect, every batch and signs. Also show error for zero and the fit mean: beating raw but losing to zero can mean mostly shrinking noise, while a fit-mean baseline checks whether the query contributes anything useful. These are cheap existing-vector controls, not extra model runs. Activation reference-mean error is a separately labelled secondary analogue, not a rescue of a failed gradient primary.

For independent identically distributed reference/query draws, reference noise contributes the same expected term to both squared errors and cancels in their difference. It still adds finite-sample uncertainty; shared documents or source shift break the simple interpretation. Filtering is not guaranteed to win: discarded mean bias competes with sampling-variance reduction. In the extreme g_i=m+ε_i with m orthogonal to the covariance span, projection removes the whole persistent mean. Conversely, a mean lying mostly in a stable low-dimensional span can benefit. This is an empirical bias/variance test, not a matrix-multiplication tautology.

Decode every raw/projected query aggregate and the unfiltered reference aggregates afterward, preserving full logits plus the unfiltered top 12 IDs/strings. Use identical positive normalization conventions only at the display boundary, never in the primary aggregate error. Report full-vocabulary directional agreement with the independent reference and all token lists descriptively; loss of discarded magnitude remains visible separately. Agreement with another J-Lens decode tests readout fidelity, **not human interpretability or semantic truth**. This answers a useful prior question before any reader study: does the filter produce a more representative aggregate to interpret? If it does not, attractive tokens cannot establish denoising. No topic vocabulary, paid judges, one-PC matching or semantic superiority score is proposed here.
