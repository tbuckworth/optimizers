# Next decision after the completed strong bridge

Codex — Spectral Optimizer Investigation · 10 September 2026, 18:16 UTC

## Decision

The selected augmentation comparison is finished, audited and reported. Carry
its useful learning and adverse combined-policy result into the paper. Do not
launch more rank/LR, corruption, multiview or early-stopping variants by default.

The next bounded task is a **design-only assessment of a component-utility
discriminator**. Determine whether the existing saved state supports a genuinely
new local mechanism question, beyond the completed six-clean-state direction/
size study. This selects mathematical and source/design work, not a new neural
acquisition, replay or paid reservation. Main can make the resulting go/no-go
decision under the user's existing autonomous authority; no new user approval
gate is being imposed.

## Why this question remains useful

The [completed result](results.md) now establishes both substantial native
post-warmup learning under corruption and consistent harm from combining the
fixed filter with translation. The [objective decomposition](../../research/spectral_augmentation_loss_geometry_2026-09-10.md)
distinguishes softened-target fitting S, fixed-assignment force F and view
consistency C. That gives a sharper question than another endpoint contest:
which locally helpful or harmful changes does the actual update restrict?

S is not automatically useful for clean prediction, and C is not a truth
detector. High retained component energy would not answer the question. The
design must relate the full actual parameter displacement to both these
component objectives and independently defined clean/readout utility. Adam
updates of separate components cannot simply be added: its nonlinear map and
inherited state act on the complete delivered gradient.

## Required design assessment, before scientific data acquisition

1. Define the estimands and the strongest constructive/adverse predictions.
   Separate signed local utility, finite-step loss changes and trajectory
   causation. A favorable consistency score alone is insufficient.
2. Inspect existing checkpoint schema and frozen sources read-only. Establish
   which states can actually be reconstructed, with no model replay during
   this design task. Do not invent intermediate checkpoints from saved logits.
3. Fix state/image/view selection without choosing the largest observed gap.
   Reused parents and outcome-informed motivation must be explicit; different
   final models cannot isolate observer history as if they were common states.
4. Compare against the completed direction/scale and observer interventions.
   State exactly what a new outcome would add, including what both signs
   would leave unresolved. If it mainly repeats those findings, decline it.
5. If worthwhile, produce one exact bounded protocol with source/state pins,
   memory/time/archive limits and a once-only execution plan. Otherwise record
   the no-go reasoning and move to manuscript/source-priority gaps.

No outcome is presumed and no intervention is selected to repair the practical
negative. The paper is not gated on obtaining a favorable diagnostic. Familiarity,
behavioral safety and J-Lens remain distinct questions; broad capabilities
speedruns remain out of the current safety-focused priority.
